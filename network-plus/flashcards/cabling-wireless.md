# Cabling and Wireless Flashcards

**Q:** What is the max distance for Cat 5e/6/6a copper Ethernet?
**A:** 100 meters (328 feet) for Cat 5e, 6, and 6a. Cat 6 is limited to 55m for 10 Gbps.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cabling, copper, domain-2

---

**Q:** What speed does Cat 6a support?
**A:** 10 Gbps at up to 100 meters
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cabling, copper, domain-2

---

**Q:** Single-mode vs multimode fiber: key differences?
**A:** Single-mode: smaller core (8-10μm), longer distance (80+ km), uses laser. Multimode: larger core (50/62.5μm), shorter distance (up to 2km), uses LED.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cabling, fiber, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is an LC connector?
**A:** Small form-factor fiber connector — most common in modern enterprise networks. Uses a latch mechanism.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cabling, fiber, connectors, domain-2

---

**Q:** What connector type is used for Ethernet?
**A:** RJ-45 (8-pin modular connector)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cabling, copper, connectors, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is an SFP+ transceiver?
**A:** Small Form-factor Pluggable Plus — hot-swappable transceiver supporting 10 Gbps. Used to connect switches to fiber or copper.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cabling, transceivers, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)?
**A:** Wireless standard using 5 GHz only, speeds up to 6.9 Gbps, uses MU-MIMO and beamforming
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** wireless, standards, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6)?
**A:** Wireless standard using 2.4/5/6 GHz, speeds up to 9.6 Gbps, uses OFDMA and improved MU-MIMO for dense environments
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** wireless, standards, domain-2

---

**Q:** WPA2 vs WPA3: key improvement?
**A:** WPA3 uses SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals) instead of PSK, providing protection against offline dictionary attacks and forward secrecy.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** wireless, security, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is the purpose of STP (Spanning Tree Protocol)?
**A:** Prevents layer 2 loops in switched networks by blocking redundant paths. Elects a root bridge and puts redundant ports into blocking state.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** switching, stp, domain-2

---

**Q:** What is LACP?
**A:** Link Aggregation Control Protocol (802.3ad) — bundles multiple physical links into one logical link for increased bandwidth and redundancy
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** switching, lacp, domain-2
