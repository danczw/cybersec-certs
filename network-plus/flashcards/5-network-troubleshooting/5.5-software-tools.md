# Software Tools Flashcards

**Q:** What does a protocol analyzer do?
**A:** Captures frames on a wired or wireless network and presents results in human-readable format
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where might packet capture capability be built in?
**A:** The device itself — switch, router, or firewall may have a capture function built into the software
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Wireshark used for?
**A:** A protocol analyzer that provides additional protocol decodes and details about entire conversations traversing the network; can view captures exported from devices
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, wireshark, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do some administrators capture all packets to disk?
**A:** For later analysis (big data analysis)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is nmap?
**A:** Network Mapper — a port scanner that finds open port numbers, operating systems, and application versions
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** nmap, port-scanner, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What type of scan does nmap perform?
**A:** An active scan — sends queries to a device and examines the results
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** nmap, port-scanner, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are four capabilities of nmap?
**A:** 1) Discover open ports on a remote device, 2) Identify OS type and version without logging in, 3) Identify services running on open ports, 4) Scripting engine to extend functionality with custom scripts
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** nmap, port-scanner, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can nmap scan more than one device at a time?
**A:** Yes — can scan a single device or an entire IP address range, building a map of IP addresses, operating systems, and services on the network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** nmap, port-scanner, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is nmap useful for finding rogue devices?
**A:** It can perform Layer 2 scans on the same subnet, making it difficult for rogue devices to hide
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** nmap, rogue-devices, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What should you do if nmap discovers unexpected open ports on a device?
**A:** Perform additional research to identify what services are running on those ports
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** nmap, port-scanner, domain-5, prof-messer

**Q:** Why are device discovery protocols useful?
**A:** Difficult to determine what devices are plugged into a switch, their VLANs, or IP addresses by looking at the front panel — discovery protocols gather this information automatically
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cdp, lldp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is CDP?
**A:** Cisco Discovery Protocol — a proprietary protocol that provides configuration information about Cisco devices without logging in
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cdp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What information does CDP provide?
**A:** IP addresses, port identification, software version, native VLAN, hardware-level interface configuration
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** cdp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is LLDP?
**A:** Link Layer Discovery Protocol — a vendor-neutral discovery protocol supported by almost all switches
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lldp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What information does LLDP provide?
**A:** MAC address, interface identification, switch name, VLAN ID
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lldp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the key difference between CDP and LLDP?
**A:** CDP is proprietary to Cisco; LLDP is vendor-neutral and supported by almost all switches
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cdp, lldp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can CDP and LLDP run on the same device simultaneously?
**A:** Yes — both can run at the same time, and third-party applications can use them to present information in consolidated form
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cdp, lldp, discovery, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do speed test sites work?
**A:** Send a lot of information over a link and measure how long it took, both in download and upload
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When are speed tests useful for troubleshooting?
**A:** Before and after network changes to compare speeds and determine if there's any difference
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you measure speed at different times of day?
**A:** More bandwidth is available during off hours than during the middle of a workday
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why are not all speed test sites equal?
**A:** Sites farther away may give different values than closer ones, and some sites are constrained by their own bandwidth
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where do the most accurate speed test results often come from?
**A:** Your local ISP's speed test site
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four third-party speed test sites.
**A:** Fast.com, SpeedOf.Me, speedtest.net, testmy.net
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** speed-test, domain-5, prof-messer
