# Switching Issues Flashcards

**Q:** Why are switching loops so dangerous at Layer 2?
**A:** At the MAC address level, there is no mechanism for counting how many times a frame is seen (no TTL equivalent). Frames in a loop circle indefinitely until a connection is unplugged.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** switching-loops, stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do broadcasts and multicasts make switching loops worse?
**A:** A single broadcast/multicast frame is transmitted out every other interface on the switch, amplifying the looping traffic
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** switching-loops, stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How is a switching loop accidentally created?
**A:** By adding a second connection between switches, creating a redundant path with no loop prevention
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** switching-loops, stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when a switching loop occurs?
**A:** Looping traffic quickly overwhelms both switches and brings the network to a halt
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** switching-loops, stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does IP have that Layer 2 switching does not for preventing loops?
**A:** IP has a TTL (Time to Live) field that prevents packets from circling indefinitely; there is nothing in the frame at Layer 2 to prevent loops
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** switching-loops, stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the purpose of Spanning Tree Protocol (STP)?
**A:** Prevents loops on the network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are Bridge Protocol Data Units (BPDUs)?
**A:** MAC layer multicast frames sent between switches that contain configuration details and inform of topology changes
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, bpdu, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which devices can see BPDU multicasts?
**A:** All switches on the local broadcast domain
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, bpdu, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How often are BPDUs sent by default?
**A:** Every 2 seconds (hello timer)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, bpdu, timers, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When does spanning tree consider a link to be down?
**A:** When three consecutive hellos are missed (6 seconds)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, bpdu, timers, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens after spanning tree detects a link is down?
**A:** Spanning tree redesigns the topology to prevent loops (unblocks alternate paths to restore connectivity)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, topology-change, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When is the root bridge elected?
**A:** When the network first starts; all bridges participate in the election
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, root-bridge, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How is the root bridge determined?
**A:** The switch with the lowest bridge ID becomes root bridge
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, root-bridge, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the valid range for manually setting a bridge ID?
**A:** 0 to 61,440
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** stp, root-bridge, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the tiebreaker if multiple switches have the same bridge ID?
**A:** The switch with the lowest MAC address becomes root bridge
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, root-bridge, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a root port?
**A:** The port on a non-root switch used to reach the root bridge
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-roles, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a designated port?
**A:** An active port passing traffic
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-roles, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a blocked port?
**A:** A port blocked by spanning tree to prevent loops
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-roles, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the five STP port states?
**A:** Blocking, Listening, Learning, Forwarding, Disabled
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens in the STP blocking state?
**A:** Not passing traffic; preventing loops
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens in the STP listening state?
**A:** Listening for other switches on the broadcast domain before making changes
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens in the STP learning state?
**A:** Adding information to the MAC address table
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens in the STP forwarding state?
**A:** Actively passing traffic
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the STP disabled state?
**A:** Administratively turned off by the network administrator
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, port-states, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A device has an IP address but cannot communicate with others on the same network. What switching issue should you check?
**A:** VLAN misconfiguration — the interface may be assigned to the wrong VLAN
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an access port in terms of VLANs?
**A:** A switch port assigned a single VLAN ID
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do you resolve a VLAN assignment issue?
**A:** Change the VLAN ID on the interface, or move the device to an interface already configured for the correct VLAN
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Routing and VLAN configuration are correct, but packets are still being dropped. What should you check?
**A:** Access control lists (ACLs) on switches and routers — they may be blocking traffic on interfaces
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should more granular ACL rules be placed at the top of the list?
**A:** The ACL stops evaluating after finding a match, so granular rules at the top fire before broader controls lower down
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, best-practices, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should more common matches be placed at the top of an ACL?
**A:** For efficiency — the ACL stops evaluating after a match, so common matches at the top reduce processing
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, best-practices, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you disable ACL functionality before making changes?
**A:** It is possible to make a change to an ACL that effectively removes your own access to the switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, best-practices, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the default behavior on many devices when you add an ACL to an interface?
**A:** Implicitly deny all traffic not specifically listed in the ACL
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, best-practices, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if you add an empty ACL to an interface?
**A:** It effectively filters all communication through that device (implicit deny with no permit rules)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, best-practices, domain-5, prof-messer
