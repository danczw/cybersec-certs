# Wireless Issues Flashcards

**Q:** Why is interference a significant problem on wireless networks?
**A:** Limited frequencies available, only one device can communicate at a time, and multiple wireless networks in the same area can create interference
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do recent wireless standards handle interference?
**A:** They can automatically find the best frequencies and avoid those already in use by other devices; access points can detect interference and move to a better frequency
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How many non-overlapping channels are available on 2.4 GHz in North America?
**A:** Three (channels 1, 6, and 11)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** channel-management, 2.4ghz, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why might 5 GHz be preferred over 2.4 GHz in a busy area?
**A:** 5 GHz has many more frequencies available, giving a better chance of avoiding interference (2.4 GHz only has 3 non-overlapping channels in North America)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** channel-management, 5ghz, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What advantage does the 6 GHz band offer for wireless networks?
**A:** Many more frequencies available to avoid conflicts and interference
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** channel-management, 6ghz, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you disable legacy/low-speed wireless support on an access point?
**A:** Older standards slow down the entire network; best throughput comes from the most recent standards
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** channel-management, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What problem occurs if you configure a 2.4 GHz access point on channel 8?
**A:** It overlaps with both channel 6 and channel 11, reducing throughput for all devices on those channels
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** channel-management, channel-overlap, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the three non-overlapping channels to use on 2.4 GHz in the US?
**A:** Channels 1, 6, and 11
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** channel-management, 2.4ghz, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you consider enabling automatic channel selection?
**A:** So the access point can move to a more appropriate channel when available, avoiding conflicts with new access points that may have appeared
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** channel-management, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a common source of wireless interference?
**A:** Other access points — especially those with output power set to maximum while covering a small area
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can you reduce interference from an access point that covers a small area?
**A:** Turn down the output power — still provides wireless access to nearby devices while limiting interference for other access points
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can splitting a wireless network into smaller cells help?
**A:** Access points are separated from each other using different frequencies, still providing wireless access while reducing interference
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is attenuation on a wireless network?
**A:** The weakening of signal the farther you move from the access point
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** attenuation, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do administrators measure wireless attenuation?
**A:** Walk around the facility with a Wi-Fi analyzer to find where signal is best and worst
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** attenuation, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are four solutions to improve wireless signal affected by attenuation?
**A:** 1) Increase power output on the access point, 2) Add an external antenna or antenna with additional gain, 3) Limit cable length between access point and antenna, 4) Check cable for damage that could leak signal
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** attenuation, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does an external antenna with additional gain improve wireless coverage?
**A:** Increases transmission distance with the same amount of power from the access point
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** attenuation, antennas, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you limit cable length between the access point and antenna?
**A:** Higher frequencies lose more signal in coax; shorter cable means less signal loss
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** attenuation, antennas, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a wireless heat map?
**A:** A visual representation from a Wi-Fi analyzer showing signal strength — brighter/warmer colors indicate better signal, cooler colors indicate worse signal
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** site-survey, heat-map, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you perform multiple site surveys throughout the year?
**A:** Wireless infrastructure changes over time — neighboring organizations add or modify their networks
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** site-survey, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why check where neighboring organizations' access points are located?
**A:** To avoid placing your access points where they create interference; also to see what frequencies they use (may open an entire band like 6 GHz for you)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** site-survey, wireless-interference, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a client disassociation attack?
**A:** A denial of service exploiting older unprotected 802.11 management frames to remove devices from the network
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** disassociation, security, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the symptoms of a client disassociation attack?
**A:** Device constantly disconnects and reconnects, or cannot connect to the network at all
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** disassociation, security, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do you detect a client disassociation attack?
**A:** Capture frames using an 802.11 packet capture device to see disassociation frames in the traffic
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** disassociation, security, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do you prevent client disassociation attacks?
**A:** Upgrade to the latest 802.11 standards which prevent this type of attack
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** disassociation, security, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What allows devices to seamlessly roam between multiple access points?
**A:** All access points configured with the same SSID and exactly the same configuration (security features and other details)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** roaming, ssid, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if a user roams to an access point that is not configured identically?
**A:** They are dropped from the network and must reconnect
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** roaming, ssid, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is required for seamless roaming across multiple access points?
**A:** Identical configuration across all access points — same SSID, security features, and configuration details
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** roaming, ssid, domain-5, prof-messer
