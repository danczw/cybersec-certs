# Hardware Issues Flashcards

**Q:** What is Power over Ethernet (PoE)?
**A:** Provides power over the same wire used for data, simplifying installation by eliminating the need for a separate power cable
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** poe, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are three common devices that use PoE?
**A:** Telephones, access points, cameras
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** poe, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an endspan PoE power source?
**A:** Power built into the switch itself (a PoE switch)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, endspan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a midspan PoE power source?
**A:** A separate device (injector) between the switch and PoE device that injects power onto the wires
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, midspan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the DC power output and maximum current of PoE?
**A:** 15.4 watts, 350 mA
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the DC power output and maximum current of PoE+?
**A:** 25.5 watts, 600 mA
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the DC power output and maximum current of PoE++ Type 3?
**A:** 51 watts, 600 mA
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the DC power output and maximum current of PoE++ Type 4?
**A:** 71.3 watts, 960 mA
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What typical devices use standard PoE (15.4W)?
**A:** Simple telephones, small access points
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What typical devices use PoE+ (25.5W)?
**A:** Larger telephones, cameras
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What typical devices use PoE++ (51W or 71.3W)?
**A:** Laptops, PTZ cameras
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What additional Ethernet standards does PoE++ support?
**A:** 2.5-gigabit, 5-gigabit, and 10-gigabit
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, standards, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can a PoE+ switch power a PoE++ device?
**A:** No — the switch and device must use compatible PoE standards
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** poe, compatibility, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Do all interfaces on a PoE switch necessarily support the same PoE standard?
**A:** No — some switches have all interfaces PoE-capable, while others have a mix of PoE, PoE+, and PoE++ ports
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, compatibility, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a PoE power budget on a switch?
**A:** The maximum total PoE power the switch can provide (e.g., 200W or 720W). All connected devices' maximum power draw must be under this capacity.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** poe, compatibility, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What do transceivers provide for Ethernet devices?
**A:** Modular connections for Ethernet devices
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** transceivers, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What must match between a fiber transceiver and the fiber it connects to?
**A:** The wavelength — marked on the transceiver (e.g., 850 nm or 1310 nm). All wavelengths must match throughout the entire link.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, mismatch, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when transceivers are mismatched?
**A:** Signal loss, increasing error counters, loss of signal, or network slowdown
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, mismatch, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why are transceiver mismatches easy to make?
**A:** Transceivers look nearly identical — wavelength markings are very small. Once plugged into a switch, markings are no longer visible.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** transceivers, mismatch, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can you identify a transceiver's wavelength once it's plugged into a switch?
**A:** Check the switch specifications or physically remove the transceiver to read the label
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, mismatch, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a transceiver's sensitivity level?
**A:** The minimum signal it can receive and still interpret properly
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When is calculating a power budget especially important?
**A:** For long runs with many connections in the middle
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What unit is transmitted power measured in for a power budget calculation?
**A:** dBm (decibels per milliwatt)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the four steps to calculate a power budget?
**A:** 1) Determine transmitted power (dBm), 2) Calculate signal loss from media distance and connectors/splices, 3) Subtract total signal loss from transmitted power = received power, 4) Compare received power to receiver sensitivity value
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What two factors contribute to signal loss in a power budget calculation?
**A:** Media distance (fiber length) and connectors/splices (each reduces signal)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why are sensitivity values negative numbers?
**A:** Because signal decreases through media
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do you determine if received power is sufficient?
**A:** If received power ≥ sensitivity value → good signal. If received power < sensitivity value → insufficient signal.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A transceiver has a receiver sensitivity of -17 dBm. Is a received power of -14 dBm acceptable?
**A:** Yes — -14 dBm is higher than the -17 dBm sensitivity, so the signal is sufficient
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A transceiver has a receiver sensitivity of -17 dBm. Is a received power of -20 dBm acceptable?
**A:** No — -20 dBm is lower than the -17 dBm sensitivity, so there is not enough signal
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** transceivers, power-budget, domain-5, prof-messer
