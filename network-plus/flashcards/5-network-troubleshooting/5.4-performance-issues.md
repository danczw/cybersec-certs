# Performance Issues Flashcards

**Q:** What causes network congestion?
**A:** Multiple links sending more traffic than the destination link can handle (e.g., two 1-Gbps links sending to a single 1-Gbps destination)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** congestion, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens to excess packets during congestion?
**A:** They queue into a buffer, but buffers in switches/routers are relatively small — once the buffer fills, packets are discarded
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** congestion, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the two ways to resolve network congestion?
**A:** Increase the size/speed of the network, or decrease the amount of traffic
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** congestion, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does "the network is slow" usually indicate?
**A:** A bottleneck somewhere is causing a slowdown
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** bottlenecks, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are four potential sources of a bottleneck?
**A:** System bus speed, CPU speed in a switch or router, storage drive speed (HDD vs SSD), network link speeds across different locations
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** bottlenecks, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When looking at bandwidth across multiple links between two devices, which link is likely the bottleneck?
**A:** The slowest link — it holds up throughput for all other networks
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** bottlenecks, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why are bottlenecks difficult to troubleshoot?
**A:** The problem may involve many different technologies; sometimes obvious, but often requires drilling into system details
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** bottlenecks, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is bandwidth percentage?
**A:** A measure of how much a network is being used over a particular amount of time, presented as a percentage
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** bandwidth, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is throughput?
**A:** How much data was moved through the network during a time frame
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** throughput, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between bandwidth percentage and throughput?
**A:** Bandwidth percentage shows how much of the network capacity is in use (as a percentage). Throughput shows how much actual data was moved in a time frame.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** bandwidth, throughput, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are three methods to monitor bandwidth statistics?
**A:** Gather directly from a switch, use SNMP, or use NetFlow
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** bandwidth, snmp, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is latency?
**A:** The delay between the request and the response
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** latency, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Will there always be some latency on a connection?
**A:** Yes — it takes time to move information between devices; some latency is always present
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** latency, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How should you ideally measure latency on a network?
**A:** Measure response times at every stop along the path, allowing delays to be broken down into smallest parts per segment
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** latency, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What granularity do packet captures provide for latency measurement?
**A:** Microsecond granularity — shows exactly how long a packet stayed in a device, traversed a network, and forwarded to the next segment
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** latency, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is packet loss (or discard)?
**A:** A packet with no errors is discarded for other reasons before reaching its destination
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** packet-loss, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are four causes of packet loss?
**A:** Network outage, contention (not enough bandwidth), bad wireless network, bad cable causing corruption
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** packet-loss, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when a bad cable corrupts data during transmission?
**A:** Corrupted data is identified at the other side, discarded, and must be re-sent — taking additional time and resources, causing significant application delays
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** packet-loss, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What types of traffic are very sensitive to network delay?
**A:** VoIP calls and live video streams
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** jitter, voip, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when a packet is lost during real-time communication (VoIP/live video)?
**A:** It cannot be retransmitted — must be discarded and continue forward. Effects include clicking noise on phone calls or stutter on live video streams.
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** jitter, voip, packet-loss, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is jitter?
**A:** The variation in time between received frames
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** jitter, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does low jitter look like?
**A:** Packets received at regular intervals with small, consistent delay between them
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** jitter, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does high jitter look like?
**A:** Bursts of packets followed by long delays (e.g., three packets quickly, long gap, three more packets)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** jitter, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the effects of high jitter on real-time communication?
**A:** Problems hearing on phone calls and stutter during live video
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** jitter, voip, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How should packets ideally arrive for VoIP and live video?
**A:** At regular, predictable intervals
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** jitter, voip, domain-5, prof-messer
