# Cable Issues Flashcards

**Q:** What is the difference between multimode and single mode fiber?
**A:** Multimode uses multiple light paths (modes) through the fiber; single mode uses a single light path. Single mode has a much smaller core diameter.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** fiber, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the three common fiber core sizes?
**A:** 50 micron (multimode), 62.5 micron (multimode), and ~9 micron (single mode)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** fiber, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the total diameter (core + cladding) of all common fiber types?
**A:** 125 microns — all three types feel identical in hand
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** fiber, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if you mix multimode and single mode fiber on a link?
**A:** Signal errors and difficulty communicating across that link
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** fiber, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can you verify what type of fiber you have?
**A:** Check the writing on the outside of the fiber (type and size); color coding exists but cannot always be relied upon
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** fiber, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What organization standardizes copper cable construction and testing?
**A:** TIA (Telecommunications Industry Association)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, tia, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does a cable earn a specific category rating?
**A:** It is manufactured to TIA standards and passes their qualification tests
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, tia, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the minimum cable category and max distance for 1000BASE-T?
**A:** Category 5, 100 meters
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, distance, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the minimum cable category for 10GBASE-T with unshielded cable, and what is its max distance?
**A:** Category 6, 55 meters
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, distance, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What cable category supports 10GBASE-T at 100 meters?
**A:** Category 6A (augmented)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, distance, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What cable category supports 40GBASE-T, and what is its max distance?
**A:** Category 8 (shielded only), 30 meters
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, distance, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between bandwidth and throughput?
**A:** Bandwidth is the theoretical maximum data rate (size of the pipe); throughput is the actual data transferred in a time frame (water flowing through the pipe)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** bandwidth, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What units are bandwidth and throughput measured in?
**A:** Both often measured in bits per second; throughput is sometimes also measured in bytes per second
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** bandwidth, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does a cable tester do?
**A:** Validates installed cables by running tests similar to TIA category qualification; reports which category the cable matches
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** testing, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if you use the wrong category of cable for a standard?
**A:** Errors on the link causing slowdown, often due to signal attenuation or signal loss; may see CRC errors
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** testing, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does UTP stand for, and what does it look like?
**A:** Unshielded Twisted Pair — four pairs of wires with no shield, each pair twisted together
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** utp, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does STP stand for, and how does it differ from UTP?
**A:** Shielded Twisted Pair — has a shield around the entire cable and/or individual pairs, plus a grounding wire; more resistant to electrical interference
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** stp, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is crosstalk (XT)?
**A:** Signal from one pair of wires leaking into another pair of wires in the same cable
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is NEXT?
**A:** Near-End Crosstalk — measures how much signal leaks into other wires at the end closest to the testing device; usually the strongest signal point
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is FEXT?
**A:** Far-End Crosstalk — measures crosstalk at the far end of the cable from the testing device
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is alien crosstalk?
**A:** Interference transmitted from other nearby cables (not from within the same cable)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is ACR (Attenuation to Crosstalk Ratio)?
**A:** A comparison of signal loss (attenuation) versus near-end crosstalk; effectively a signal-to-noise ratio
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does a 10:1 signal-to-noise ratio mean vs. a 1:1 ratio?
**A:** 10:1 = signal is 10x stronger than noise (good); 1:1 = signal and noise are equal strength (bad)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name three ways to reduce crosstalk.
**A:** Re-terminate the cable, maintain as many twists as possible (untwist minimally), use Cat 6A cable (has spacer between pairs)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** crosstalk, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is EMI?
**A:** Electromagnetic Interference — electrical interference that can affect signals in both UTP and STP cables
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** emi, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if there is a break in an STP cable's shield?
**A:** Electromagnetic interference can get into the wires at that point
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** emi, stp, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four sources of EMI to avoid when running cables.
**A:** Power cords, fluorescent lights, electrical systems/generators, fire prevention components
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** emi, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you avoid staples and limit plastic cable ties on network cables?
**A:** They can crimp the wires inside; use Velcro ties instead
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** emi, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is attenuation?
**A:** Loss of signal strength over distance; occurs in both copper and fiber optic connections
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** attenuation, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do IEEE standards specify maximum cable distances?
**A:** Because of attenuation — signal strength weakens over distance, so there is a maximum length that still maintains acceptable signal quality
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** attenuation, distance, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is proper pin-to-pin matching in a cable termination?
**A:** Pin 1 on one end connects to pin 1 on the other end, pin 2 to pin 2, pin 3 to pin 3, etc.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** termination, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What can happen if pins are mismatched during cable termination?
**A:** Speed drops (e.g., expecting 1 Gbps but only getting 100 Mbps) or complete signal loss
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** termination, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are crossed pairs in a cable?
**A:** A wiring error where some pins connect to the wrong pins (e.g., pin 1 to pin 3, pin 3 to pin 1) — half crossed, half straight through
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** termination, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where can crossed pairs occur?
**A:** At the RJ45 crimp or at the punchdown block — check every connection along the path
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** termination, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Auto-MDIX?
**A:** A capability where the ethernet chipset detects a cable cross and reverses it electronically, effectively turning it back into a straight-through cable
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** termination, auto-mdix, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Should you rely on Auto-MDIX to fix crossed cables?
**A:** No — it's not always enabled or supported on every chipset; best practice is to properly terminate cables
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** termination, auto-mdix, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can you easily detect crossed pairs in a cable?
**A:** Use a cable tester — it will clearly show which pins are crossed
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** testing, termination, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does minimum bend radius refer to?
**A:** The tightest bend you can make in a cable without causing damage; check cable documentation for the specification
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** emi, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Category 7 cable has what requirement that Category 6 does not?
**A:** Category 7 is shielded only (STP required)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** copper, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What do CRC errors on a link typically indicate?
**A:** Physical layer problems (cable issues causing data corruption)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** testing, cable-issues, domain-5, prof-messer
