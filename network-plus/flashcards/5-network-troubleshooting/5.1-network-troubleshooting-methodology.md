# Network Troubleshooting Methodology Flashcards

**Q:** What are the 7 steps of the network troubleshooting methodology (in order)?
**A:** 1) Identify the problem, 2) Establish a theory of probable cause, 3) Test the theory, 4) Establish a plan of action, 5) Implement the solution, 6) Verify full system functionality, 7) Document findings
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** troubleshooting, methodology, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the first step in the troubleshooting methodology?
**A:** Identify the problem
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, methodology, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four things you should do when identifying a network problem.
**A:** Attempt to duplicate the issue, talk to users, review statistics/metrics from routers and switches, determine what has changed
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you attempt to duplicate a network problem?
**A:** If you can duplicate it, it's easier to find the root cause and ultimately resolve the issue
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What question should you always ask when identifying a network problem?
**A:** What has changed since it last worked? (cables moved, system powered off, configuration changes)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why might you build a lab when troubleshooting?
**A:** To attempt to duplicate the problem in a controlled environment, making it easier to find the root cause
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What should you do if the scope of a problem is too large?
**A:** Break it into smaller pieces and take one component at a time to determine where the problem is really happening
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When establishing a theory of probable cause, what should you start with?
**A:** Obvious problems and problems that can be solved very quickly (e.g., swap a cable)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, theory, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the top-down troubleshooting approach?
**A:** Start at the application layer (Layer 7) and work down through the OSI model toward the physical layer — good for existing networks
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, theory, osi, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the bottom-up troubleshooting approach?
**A:** Start at the physical layer (Layer 1) and work up through the OSI model toward the application — good for new implementations
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, theory, osi, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can you use elimination to narrow down a probable cause?
**A:** If the issue appears across different variables (e.g., multiple operating systems), you can eliminate that variable as the cause
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, theory, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What do you do if testing your theory does NOT fix the problem?
**A:** Go back to establishing a new theory of probable cause and repeat the process
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where should you test configuration changes before applying them to production?
**A:** In a lab environment
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, test, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is change control in the context of troubleshooting?
**A:** A process that schedules and manages changes to the production network, often requiring a specific maintenance window
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, plan, change-control, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What contingencies should a plan of action include?
**A:** Plan B (and Plan C) if the primary plan fails, plus a rollback process to revert to the previous state
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, plan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is a rollback process important in a plan of action?
**A:** In case the implementation causes additional problems, you need a way to go back to the way things were before you started
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, plan, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In some organizations, who implements the fix vs. who determines what the fix should be?
**A:** The troubleshooting team determines what needs to be changed, and the operations team makes the actual change
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, implement, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When is a fix truly confirmed as working?
**A:** When end users verify that the problem they saw before is now resolved — not just when the change is made
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, verify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What should you discuss with users after verifying the fix works?
**A:** Ways to prevent the problem from occurring in the future (both workflow improvements and technology-based prevention)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, verify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What should be documented at the end of the troubleshooting process?
**A:** The process followed and the specific change that resolved the issue
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, document, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is troubleshooting documentation typically stored?
**A:** Help desk database or knowledge base
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, document, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is documentation important in the troubleshooting methodology?
**A:** If the same problem recurs in the future, you can refer back to see exactly what was done to resolve it
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, document, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What step immediately follows "Test the theory" if the theory is confirmed?
**A:** Establish a plan of action (to implement the fix in production)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** troubleshooting, methodology, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why can't you always implement a fix immediately after confirming it works in the lab?
**A:** Many production networks cannot be touched during business hours and require scheduling a change control window
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, plan, change-control, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What two sources of information should you combine when identifying a problem?
**A:** User experiences/observations and statistics/metrics gathered from routers and switches
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is it important to consider multiple symptoms rather than just one?
**A:** Multiple symptoms may be working together to cause the issue — focusing on a single symptom may miss the real problem
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** troubleshooting, identify, domain-5, prof-messer
