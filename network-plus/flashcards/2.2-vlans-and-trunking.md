# VLANs and Trunking Flashcards

**Q:** What is a LAN in terms of broadcast domains?
**A:** A group of devices in the same broadcast domain
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, lan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What defines a broadcast domain on a switch?
**A:** All devices connected to a switch are in the same broadcast domain
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, lan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can devices on separate, unconnected switches communicate directly?
**A:** No — separate switches that are not connected are separate broadcast domains
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, lan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a VLAN?
**A:** A Virtual Local Area Network — separate broadcast domains on the same physical switch
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How are VLANs identified?
**A:** By number (VLAN 1, VLAN 2, VLAN 3, etc.)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can devices in different VLANs on the same switch communicate directly?
**A:** No — only devices in the same VLAN can communicate with each other
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the benefits of VLANs over multiple physical switches?
**A:** Reduces cost, space, power, and management — one switch instead of multiple
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What problem does 802.1Q trunking solve?
**A:** Connecting VLANs across multiple switches would require one cable per VLAN; a trunk uses a single physical connection for all VLANs
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a VLAN trunk?
**A:** A single physical connection that carries traffic for all VLANs between switches
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does 802.1Q trunking work (3 steps)?
**A:** 1) Device sends normal ethernet frame to the switch 2) Switch adds VLAN tag before sending across trunk 3) Receiving switch reads tag, removes it, delivers frame to correct VLAN
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is the VLAN tag inserted in an ethernet frame?
**A:** After the source MAC address
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How many bits is a VLAN tag?
**A:** 12 bits
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How many total usable VLANs does 802.1Q support?
**A:** 4,094
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the "normal range" for VLAN IDs?
**A:** VLAN 1–1005
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the "extended range" for VLAN IDs?
**A:** VLAN 1006–4094
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which VLAN numbers are reserved?
**A:** VLAN 0 and 4095
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the fields of a normal ethernet frame?
**A:** Preamble, SFD, Destination MAC, Source MAC, Type, Payload, FCS
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, ethernet, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the fields of an 802.1Q tagged frame?
**A:** Preamble, SFD, Destination MAC, Source MAC, VLAN, Type, Payload, FCS
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is ISL?
**A:** Inter-Switch Link — a proprietary (Cisco) trunking protocol that predates 802.1Q; relatively outdated
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, isl, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What trunking standard will you likely encounter today?
**A:** 802.1Q — ISL is relatively outdated
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, 802.1q, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the default VLAN on a switch?
**A:** The VLAN all interfaces belong to until reconfigured (commonly VLAN 1)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a native VLAN?
**A:** A VLAN whose traffic traverses a trunk WITHOUT a VLAN tag
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, native-vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the native VLAN used for?
**A:** Devices that cannot communicate over 802.1Q, and management traffic / switch notification messages
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, native-vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if the native VLAN is different on each side of a trunk?
**A:** Error messages appear in the switch log
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, trunking, native-vlan, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a layer 3 switch?
**A:** A device that combines layer 2 switching (MAC-based forwarding) and layer 3 routing (IP-based forwarding) in one unit
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What can a layer 3 switch do that a layer 2 switch cannot?
**A:** Route between VLANs within the same switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are SVIs?
**A:** Switched Virtual Interfaces — the inter-VLAN routing interfaces on a layer 3 switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, svi, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What may be required to enable layer 3 functionality on a switch?
**A:** Enable it in switch configuration; often requires a switch restart
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a limitation of layer 3 switches compared to standalone routers?
**A:** Routing functionality tends to be less capable than a standalone router
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where are layer 3 switches most appropriate?
**A:** Smaller/remote sites; larger networks still benefit from dedicated routers
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, layer-3-switch, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do voice and data need separate VLANs?
**A:** Data uses bursty bandwidth; voice requires consistent access and is affected by congestion — separating them prevents interference
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, voice, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How are voice and data separated on a single cable?
**A:** Voice on one VLAN (e.g., VLAN 200), data on another (e.g., VLAN 100); switch uses trunking on the same cable
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, voice, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In a voice/data VLAN setup, what is the physical connection order?
**A:** Single cable from switch → phone → computer
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, voice, domain-2, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does a switch need to support for voice and data VLANs on one port?
**A:** Must support recognizing both phone and computer on the same port
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vlan, voice, domain-2, prof-messer
