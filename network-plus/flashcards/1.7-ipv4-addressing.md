# IPv4 Addressing Flashcards

**Q:** What is an IP address?
**A:** A unique identifier every device on the network must have to communicate using the IP protocol
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, fundamentals, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How many bits are in an IPv4 address?
**A:** 32 (4 octets × 8 bits)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, fundamentals, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the maximum decimal value of a single IPv4 octet?
**A:** 255
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, fundamentals, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** At what OSI layer does IPv4 operate?
**A:** Layer 3 (network layer)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, fundamentals, osi, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the purpose of a subnet mask?
**A:** Used with the IP address to determine what subnet a device is on — identifies which addresses are local vs. remote
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, subnet-mask, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Is the subnet mask transmitted across the network?
**A:** No — it's used locally to determine what's on the local subnet vs. outside it
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, subnet-mask, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a default gateway?
**A:** The IP address of the router on your local subnet that allows communication outside the subnet
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, default-gateway, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the requirement for a default gateway address?
**A:** It must be an IP address on your local network
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, default-gateway, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the IPv4 loopback address range?
**A:** 127.0.0.1 through 127.255.255.254
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, loopback, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the loopback address used for?
**A:** To confirm the local IP stack is working properly — references the local device without needing its actual IP
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, loopback, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the reserved (Class E) address range?
**A:** 240.0.0.1 through 254.255.255.254 — set aside for future use/testing, never assigned to devices
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ipv4, reserved, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a Virtual IP address (VIP)?
**A:** An IP address not associated with a physical Ethernet adapter — assigned to internals of a device (VMs, router interfaces)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, vip, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does DHCP do?
**A:** Automatically assigns IP address, subnet mask, and default gateway to devices on the network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, dhcp, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is APIPA?
**A:** Automatic Private IP Addressing — assigns a link-local address when no DHCP server is available
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the APIPA address range?
**A:** 169.254.0.1 through 169.254.255.254
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the functional APIPA range actually assigned to devices?
**A:** 169.254.1.0 through 169.254.254.255 — first and last 256 addresses are reserved
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the limitation of an APIPA address?
**A:** Link-local only — can communicate on the local subnet but cannot route to the internet or other subnets
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does APIPA ensure the chosen address is unique?
**A:** Uses ARP to confirm no other device on the local network has that address
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, arp, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why were private IP address ranges created?
**A:** IPv4 exhausted all available public address blocks — private ranges extend usability on internal networks
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, private, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can private IP addresses be routed on the public internet?
**A:** No — they require NAT to convert to a public address for internet communication
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, private, nat, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What RFC defines private IP address ranges?
**A:** RFC 1918
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, private, rfc, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the 10.x.x.x private range in CIDR notation?
**A:** 10.0.0.0/8 (subnet mask 255.0.0.0, 24 host bits, 16M+ addresses)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, private, cidr, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the 172.16.x.x private range in CIDR notation?
**A:** 172.16.0.0/12 (subnet mask 255.240.0.0, 20 host bits, 1M+ addresses)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ipv4, private, cidr, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the 192.168.x.x private range in CIDR notation?
**A:** 192.168.0.0/16 (subnet mask 255.255.0.0, 16 host bits, 65K+ addresses)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, private, cidr, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the upper boundary of the 172.16.0.0/12 private range?
**A:** 172.31.255.255
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ipv4, private, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** You plug a laptop into a network and receive the address 169.254.47.12. What happened?
**A:** No DHCP server responded — the device self-assigned an APIPA link-local address
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ipv4, apipa, scenario, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A device has IP 10.4.2.50. Can it communicate directly on the internet without NAT?
**A:** No — 10.x.x.x is a private range and cannot be routed on the public internet
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, private, nat, scenario, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What three values does a device need to communicate outside its local subnet?
**A:** IP address, subnet mask, and default gateway
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ipv4, fundamentals, domain-1, prof-messer
