# Network Topologies Flashcards

**Q:** What is the most popular topology in large networks?
**A:** Star (hub-and-spoke)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, star, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In a star topology, how do devices communicate?
**A:** All communication passes through the central device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, star, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a common example of a star topology in a LAN?
**A:** Switched Ethernet — a switch in the middle with all devices connected to it
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, star, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is another name for star topology?
**A:** Hub-and-spoke
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, star, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a mesh topology?
**A:** Each device/location connects via multiple network paths
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens in a mesh topology when one link fails?
**A:** Traffic uses an alternate path
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does mesh topology enable by having multiple connections?
**A:** Load balancing — traffic can be split across multiple connections
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is mesh topology most commonly deployed?
**A:** WANs — to maintain connectivity regardless of link availability
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can mesh topology be used in LANs?
**A:** Yes, but it is less common than in WANs
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a point-to-point topology?
**A:** A single point connected to a single point — a direct link between two endpoints
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, point-to-point, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are examples of point-to-point connections in older WANs?
**A:** Point-to-point T1 or T3 connections
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, point-to-point, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How is point-to-point used in LANs?
**A:** Connecting buildings on a campus
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, point-to-point, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a hybrid topology?
**A:** A network that combines multiple topology types in one enterprise
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, hybrid, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give an example of a hybrid topology.
**A:** Star in one part, point-to-point in another, mesh in a third — different sections have different needs
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, hybrid, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is spine and leaf topology used?
**A:** Data centers
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the two tiers of switches in spine and leaf?
**A:** Spine switches at the top and leaf switches in the middle (devices at the bottom)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In spine and leaf, what is the connectivity rule for leaf switches?
**A:** Every leaf connects to every spine, but leaf switches do NOT connect directly to each other
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In spine and leaf, do spine switches connect directly to each other?
**A:** No — spine switches do NOT connect directly to each other
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is top-of-rack switching in a spine and leaf topology?
**A:** A leaf switch at the top of each physical rack — all devices in the rack connect to their rack's leaf switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the cabling advantage of top-of-rack switching?
**A:** Cabling is self-contained within each rack
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does spine and leaf provide redundancy?
**A:** Multiple spine connections from each leaf switch — if one spine fails, alternate paths exist
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the maximum number of switch hops in a spine and leaf topology?
**A:** One — never more than one switch hop from any other device
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the cost tradeoff of spine and leaf?
**A:** Cost scales with number of racks — each rack requires its own separate leaf switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which topology maintains connectivity regardless of individual link failures?
**A:** Mesh — multiple paths ensure alternate routes exist
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which topology is most common in enterprise networks?
**A:** Hybrid — large enterprises use different topologies in different sections
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, hybrid, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** You need to connect two buildings on a campus with a single link. What topology is this?
**A:** Point-to-point
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, point-to-point, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** You're designing a data center and need predictable low-latency between any two servers. What topology should you use?
**A:** Spine and leaf — never more than one switch hop between devices
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** You're designing a WAN that must stay up even if individual links fail. What topology should you use?
**A:** Mesh — multiple paths between locations provide redundancy
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A network has a central switch with all workstations connected to it. What topology is this?
**A:** Star (hub-and-spoke)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, star, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A network combines star in the office, mesh in the WAN, and point-to-point between campuses. What topology is this?
**A:** Hybrid
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** topology, hybrid, domain-1, prof-messer

**Q:** In spine and leaf, which layer do end devices connect to?
**A:** Leaf switches — end devices never connect directly to spine switches
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, spine-and-leaf, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What two benefits does mesh topology provide?
**A:** Redundancy (alternate paths if a link fails) and load balancing (split traffic across connections)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** topology, mesh, domain-1, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do large networks reflect hybrid topologies?
**A:** Different sections have different needs — no single topology fits all requirements
