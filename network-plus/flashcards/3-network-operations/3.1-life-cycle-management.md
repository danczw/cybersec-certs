# Life Cycle Management Flashcards

**Q:** What does End of Life (EOL) mean?
**A:** The manufacturer no longer supports a product — no new software versions or feature enhancements, though security patches may continue
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, eol, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does End of Support (EOS) mean?
**A:** No patches, updates, new features, or anything from the manufacturer — ever
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, eos, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is EOS more urgent than EOL?
**A:** EOS means no future security updates ever — a significant security concern; EOL may still receive security patches
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, eol, eos, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What should you do when a product reaches EOL?
**A:** Put together a plan and budget for replacing that product over time
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, eol, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What two things do patches and bug fixes maintain?
**A:** Uptime/availability/stability, and they close security holes
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, patches, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How do many organizations schedule updates?
**A:** Monthly updates on a recurring schedule — allows planning deployment around known time frames
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, patches, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When might a manufacturer release an update outside the normal schedule?
**A:** For a zero-day or significant security event
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, patches, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four operating systems that receive constant updates.
**A:** Windows, Linux, iOS, Android
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, os-updates, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Besides bug fixes and security patches, what internal changes might be deployed to operating systems?
**A:** User account modifications, password length/complexity changes, access method changes, firewall configurations (IP/URL), anti-malware/antivirus updates
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** lifecycle, os-updates, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is firmware?
**A:** Software running on a purpose-built appliance/hardware (printers, cable modems, etc.) with its own embedded OS
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, firmware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why are firmware updates important?
**A:** They close security holes on devices where you don't have direct OS access
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, firmware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can firmware updates be performed?
**A:** Over the network or by physically connecting to the device
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, firmware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the best practice for firmware rollback?
**A:** Save firmware binaries in a safe place so you can revert to a previous version if the new one has problems
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, firmware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What challenge exists with firmware maintenance?
**A:** Hardware manufacturers may not maintain firmware promptly (e.g., Trane thermostat — vulnerabilities reported April 2014, final fix January 2016)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** lifecycle, firmware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is decommissioning?
**A:** The process of disposing of an asset once it's no longer needed
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, decommissioning, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What must be done to protect data when decommissioning a device?
**A:** Sanitize the media or destroy the device
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, decommissioning, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why can't you always destroy decommissioned equipment immediately?
**A:** Legal issues — certain data may not be allowed to be destroyed; may need secure storage until proper disposal
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, decommissioning, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why should you never dispose of old equipment in normal trash?
**A:** Someone will access the data or sell the equipment to someone who will
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, decommissioning, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What must be protected when recycling hardware?
**A:** The confidentiality of data on the devices
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, decommissioning, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is change management?
**A:** A managed process for tracking, monitoring, and modifying changes to software, configurations, and other systems
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, change-management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What four elements does a clearly defined change management process include?
**A:** How often changes can be made, the window for changes, the process for installing changes, and the process for rolling back
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** lifecycle, change-management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give three examples of changes that fall under change management.
**A:** Software upgrades on a switch, firewall configuration changes, router table modifications
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, change-management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is critical for change management to work?
**A:** Everyone must understand and follow the centralized change management process
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, change-management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does the help desk process work?
**A:** Receive call → input ticket into process tracking system → triage → assign to resolver → close ticket
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, help-desk, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does "triage" mean in the help desk context?
**A:** Prioritizing tickets and assigning them to someone who can resolve the problem
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** lifecycle, help-desk, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What type of system does almost every organization have for service requests?
**A:** A process tracking system (ticketing system)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** lifecycle, help-desk, domain-3, prof-messer
