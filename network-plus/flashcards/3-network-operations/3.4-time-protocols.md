# Time Protocols Flashcards

**Q:** What is the primary purpose of NTP?
**A:** Synchronizes clocks across all network devices (laptops, desktops, switches, routers, firewalls)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is time synchronization critical on a network?
**A:** To compare log files and timestamps from multiple devices accurately
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What port and protocol does NTP use?
**A:** UDP port 123
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, ports, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How accurate is NTP synchronization between devices?
**A:** Devices typically differ by only milliseconds (~10 ms on local servers)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ntp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does an NTP server update its own time?
**A:** It uses a separate NTP client to query a different NTP server — it does not modify its own time from client requests
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ntp, server, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can a single device run both an NTP client and NTP server simultaneously?
**A:** Yes — the server responds to time requests while the client queries upstream servers for updates
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ntp, client, server, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What security weakness does NTP have by default?
**A:** NTP sends information in the clear (unencrypted)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, security, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens if Kerberos client and server timestamps differ by more than 5 minutes?
**A:** Kerberos authentication fails
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ntp, kerberos, security, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How could an attacker exploit NTP to cause a denial of service?
**A:** By providing wrong time to clients, causing Kerberos authentication (and other time-dependent services) to fail
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ntp, security, attacks, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is NTS (Network Time Security)?
**A:** An extension that adds authentication to NTP so responses can be trusted
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** nts, security, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What additional server does NTS require on the network?
**A:** An NTS Key Exchange (NTS-KE) server
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** nts, security, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the two steps of the NTS authentication process?
**A:** 1) Client performs TLS handshake with NTS-KE server and receives a cookie. 2) Client includes the cookie in the NTP request to prove authentication.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** nts, security, authentication, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does the NTP server do after receiving a valid NTS cookie?
**A:** Responds with a trusted timestamp
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** nts, security, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is PTP (Precision Time Protocol)?
**A:** Hardware-based time synchronization with nanosecond granularity
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ptp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does PTP's precision compare to NTP?
**A:** PTP achieves nanosecond granularity vs. NTP's ~10 milliseconds on local servers
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ptp, ntp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What type of environments use PTP?
**A:** Industrial environments requiring very precise timestamps
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ptp, time, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why does PTP require separate dedicated hardware?
**A:** It has its own OS and runs without delays from third-party processes, providing the most accurate timestamps possible
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ptp, hardware, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the time synchronization schedule for NTP clients?
**A:** Configurable — devices can update daily, hourly, or on other schedules (automatic process)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, client, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are two options organizations have for NTP servers?
**A:** Use external NTP servers or run their own internal NTP servers
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ntp, server, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Compare NTP, NTS, and PTP in terms of purpose.
**A:** NTP: basic time sync (milliseconds). NTS: authenticated time sync (secure NTP). PTP: hardware-based time sync (nanoseconds) for industrial use.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ntp, nts, ptp, comparison, domain-3, prof-messer
