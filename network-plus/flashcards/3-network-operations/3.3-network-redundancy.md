# Network Redundancy Flashcards

**Q:** What is an active-passive configuration?
**A:** Two separate pieces of equipment where only one is active at any time — if the primary fails, the secondary takes over
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In active-passive, what do the two devices constantly do?
**A:** Communicate between each other, giving updates on their status
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when the primary device fails in an active-passive configuration?
**A:** The secondary device takes over as the primary device on the network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why must configuration between active-passive devices be identical?
**A:** So that if the secondary becomes active, it has exactly the same configuration as the primary did and can take over seamlessly
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When you make a configuration change to the primary device in active-passive, what must happen?
**A:** The configuration must be copied to the secondary device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Besides configuration files, what real-time information must be maintained on the secondary device in active-passive?
**A:** Session tables and routing tables
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In an active-passive firewall setup, what might cause the active firewall to go offline?
**A:** Power supply failure or software crash
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does the passive firewall know the active firewall has failed?
**A:** It has been in constant communication with the active firewall and recognizes it is no longer on the network
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-passive, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an active-active configuration?
**A:** Both devices operating simultaneously, handling traffic at the same time
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why isn't active-active as simple as just turning both devices on?
**A:** It requires additional engineering — you need a mechanism to track all data flows and which device they're flowing through
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What challenge arises with data flows in an active-active configuration?
**A:** Data may flow in different directions — one part of a conversation through one device and the return part through a different device
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What four things must be understood when designing an active-active configuration?
**A:** Traffic flow directions, routing configuration, switch locations, and expected normal traffic flow patterns
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when one device fails in an active-active configuration?
**A:** The remaining device continues handling all the load — no failover process is needed because both were already active
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is there no failover process in active-active when a device fails?
**A:** Both devices are already active, so the remaining one simply continues handling traffic normally
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In active-active firewalls, how is traffic distributed?
**A:** Traffic flows are split across both firewalls — one flow may go through one firewall and another flow through the second
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the key difference between active-passive and active-active?
**A:** Active-passive: only one device handles traffic, the other is on standby. Active-active: both devices handle traffic simultaneously.
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** active-passive, active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which configuration requires more engineering design work — active-passive or active-active?
**A:** Active-active — requires understanding traffic flows, routing, and a mechanism to track which device handles which data flows
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-passive, active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What advantage does active-active have over active-passive in terms of resource utilization?
**A:** Both devices are used simultaneously rather than one being on standby
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** active-passive, active-active, redundancy, domain-3, prof-messer
