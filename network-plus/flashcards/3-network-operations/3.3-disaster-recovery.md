# Disaster Recovery Flashcards

**Q:** What is a Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)?
**A:** A plan covering every aspect and detail of handling outages or significant problems affecting organizational goals
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** drp, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four technologies involved in disaster recovery.
**A:** Backups, offsite data replication, cloud-based alternatives, and a completely separate remote site
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** drp, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What two types of third-party disaster recovery services can an organization contract?
**A:** A temporary facility to move operations to, and recovery services that come in and manage the disaster recovery process
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** drp, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Recovery Time Objective (RTO)?
**A:** The amount of time to get back up and running after an outage — measured from the outage to when normal service level is restored
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rto, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the goal for RTO?
**A:** As close to zero as possible
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rto, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give an example of RTO.
**A:** A web server fails; the RTO is 1 hour for it to become available again
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rto, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Recovery Point Objective (RPO)?
**A:** The amount of data (measured in time) lost when an outage occurs — the gap between the last data recovery point and the outage
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rpo, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the goal for RPO?
**A:** As close to zero as possible
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rpo, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What determines an organization's RPO?
**A:** Available resources for data recovery and the frequency of backups being performed
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rpo, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why would banking transactions have a shorter RPO than internal documents?
**A:** Banking transactions / patient info cannot tolerate much data loss (RPO < 1 hour), while website updates / internal documents may only be backed up every 1–2 hours (longer RPO)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rpo, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** On a disaster timeline, where does RPO fall vs RTO?
**A:** RPO = time between the last data recovery point and the outage (data lost). RTO = time between the outage and services coming back online (downtime).
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rpo, rto, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does RPO measure — time or data?
**A:** Data loss, but expressed as an amount of time (how far back data was not stored/backed up)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rpo, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Mean Time to Repair (MTTR)?
**A:** The average time to resolve an issue, based on historical data across multiple incidents
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mttr, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between MTTR and RTO?
**A:** RTO is a forward-looking target (how long recovery should take). MTTR is a backward-looking statistical average (how long repairs actually take).
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** mttr, rto, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)?
**A:** The average time a device is expected to operate before failing, presented as a single time frame
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mtbf, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** A firewall has an MTBF of 20 years. How does this help disaster recovery planning?
**A:** You can plan around the long expected lifetime — e.g., only needing one backup unit instead of multiples because the device is expected to last a long time
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mtbf, recovery-metrics, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is site resiliency?
**A:** The process of moving operations from a primary data center to a temporary facility and back again
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the four steps of site resiliency?
**A:** 1) Prepare the DR site (power, hardware, data), 2) Move from primary to backup facility, 3) Work from backup facility until resolved, 4) Move assets and data back to original location
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How long might an organization work from a backup facility?
**A:** Hours to months, depending on the problem that occurred
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a cold site?
**A:** An empty building with no equipment, no data, and no people — must bring backup tapes/equipment and transport people to the location
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cold-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the main advantage of a cold site?
**A:** Least expensive option for a backup location
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cold-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the main disadvantage of a cold site?
**A:** A lot of work when disaster is called — must bring all equipment, data, and people
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cold-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a hot site?
**A:** An exact replica of the primary data center — same hardware, applications and data replicated continuously, can be up and running relatively quickly
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** hot-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why can a hot site be operational so quickly after a disaster?
**A:** No need to install hardware, install applications, or recover data from backups — everything is already in place and replicated
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** hot-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is common practice when purchasing new equipment for a data center that has a hot site?
**A:** Purchase additional units of the same hardware for the hot site at the same time
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** hot-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a warm site?
**A:** Between a cold site and hot site — has some infrastructure (power, racks, possibly some hardware) but you still need to bring data and recover from backup tapes
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** warm-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Rank cold, warm, and hot sites by cost (low to high) and recovery speed (slow to fast).
**A:** Cost: cold < warm < hot. Recovery speed: cold (slowest) < warm < hot (fastest).
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cold-site, warm-site, hot-site, site-resiliency, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a tabletop exercise?
**A:** A meeting where everyone sits around a conference table and steps through simulated disaster scenarios, describing what each person/department would do — no physical movement of backups or equipment
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** tabletop-exercise, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How long does a tabletop exercise typically take?
**A:** About a day or two
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** tabletop-exercise, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does a tabletop exercise verify?
**A:** That all logistics are in place to get data from one location to another during a disaster
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** tabletop-exercise, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a validation test in disaster recovery?
**A:** A full-blown disaster-recovery site test that follows a specific scenario (e.g., fire, evacuation), going through the same DR process without actually moving production
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** validation-test, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How often are validation tests typically performed?
**A:** Once or multiple times per year
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** validation-test, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is documented after a validation test?
**A:** What worked and what needs to be fixed — allows ongoing improvements for efficiency
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** validation-test, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give two example scenarios used in validation tests.
**A:** A fire destroys the building where the primary data center exists; everyone in the geographic area around the data center must be evacuated
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** validation-test, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a tabletop exercise and a validation test?
**A:** Tabletop is discussion-only around a conference table (1–2 days, less costly). Validation test is a full-blown DR site test going through the actual process without moving production (more costly, takes people out of normal jobs).
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** tabletop-exercise, validation-test, dr-testing, domain-3, prof-messer
