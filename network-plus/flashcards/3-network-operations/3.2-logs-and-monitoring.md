# Logs and Monitoring Flashcards

**Q:** What is NetFlow?
**A:** A summary of statistics based on traffic flows traversing the network; uses a standard collection method with products from many manufacturers
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** netflow, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the three components of a NetFlow architecture?
**A:** Probe (captures raw packets and compiles statistics), Collector (central destination storing summaries from all probes), Report generator (often built into collector; provides real-time and long-term reports)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** netflow, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does a NetFlow probe connect to the network?
**A:** Via a physical tap or a SPAN port on a switch
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** netflow, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What types of data does NetFlow provide?
**A:** Top conversations (ingress/egress), top endpoints, traffic by port/application, and events
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** netflow, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the advantage of a central NetFlow collector?
**A:** No need to go to separate probes — all reports are generated from one central place, and data is stored over time
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** netflow, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a protocol analyzer?
**A:** A tool that captures and displays raw packets (exact bits and bytes) sent across the network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What types of networks can a protocol analyzer capture from?
**A:** Both wired and wireless networks
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does a protocol analyzer present captured data?
**A:** In a human-readable summary with the ability to drill down into packet details
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are two use cases for a protocol analyzer?
**A:** Finding unknown traffic and troubleshooting slow applications
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do some organizations store all captured packets long-term?
**A:** For later analysis — stored on large storage arrays
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a network performance baseline?
**A:** A picture of what a normal day on the network looks like, built from collected data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** baseline, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How is a baseline useful for troubleshooting?
**A:** Compare current values to what's normal to determine if something is better or worse than expected
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** baseline, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What level of detail can a baseline provide?
**A:** Can drill down to individual device performance
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** baseline, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can a management console or SIEM use baseline data?
**A:** Identify anomalies by comparing current activity against the established baseline
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** baseline, siem, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is syslog?
**A:** A standard protocol for transferring log files from network devices to a central location
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** syslog, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What types of devices send syslog data?
**A:** Switches, routers, firewalls, and servers
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** syslog, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What three pieces of information does a syslog entry include?
**A:** Device identifier (which device sent it), facility code (program that created the log entry), and severity level (importance of the entry)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** syslog, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a syslog facility code?
**A:** An identifier for the program that originally created the log entry
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** syslog, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the syslog severity level?
**A:** A value indicating the importance of a log entry
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** syslog, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why would you adjust syslog sensitivity settings on a SIEM?
**A:** To only capture warning/urgent information and exclude informational details, reducing the volume of stored data
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** syslog, siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is syslog data commonly sent?
**A:** To a SIEM (Security Information and Event Manager)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** syslog, siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a SIEM?
**A:** Security Information and Event Manager — a central system collecting log files from many devices, stored on large drive arrays
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What real-time capabilities does a SIEM dashboard provide?
**A:** Alerts and alarms for events like failed authentications or devices going offline
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What reporting capabilities does a SIEM offer?
**A:** Advanced reporting — compare and contrast statistics across multiple devices over time
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an ad hoc query on a SIEM?
**A:** A custom search of log data (e.g., searching for "fail*" AND "password" to find authentication failures)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can a SIEM be used for forensics?
**A:** Go back in time to track authentication events and service usage from stored log data
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** siem, forensics, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What full traffic path can a SIEM track?
**A:** Server → switch → firewall → internet
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** siem, logging, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is API integration in network management?
**A:** A central management station communicating to devices via Application Programming Interface, eliminating the need to SSH into each device individually
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** api, management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What can API integration do that SSH cannot easily accomplish?
**A:** Scripts and programs can send commands, make configuration changes, or query information on many devices automatically with one action
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** api, management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is API integration essential for large environments?
**A:** Managing hundreds or thousands of devices individually via SSH is tedious and nearly impossible; API allows automated changes to all devices at once
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** api, management, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is port mirroring?
**A:** Redirecting a copy of traffic from one switch port to another port connected to a monitoring device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is another name for port mirroring?
**A:** Switched Port Analyzer (SPAN)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are three use cases for port mirroring/SPAN?
**A:** IDS/IPS, performance monitoring, and NetFlow collection
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is remote SPAN?
**A:** Some switches support mirroring traffic from one switch to another
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What can be mirrored with SPAN on a switch?
**A:** Single interfaces, multiple interfaces, or entire VLANs
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Does port mirroring affect the original traffic?
**A:** No — original traffic is unaffected; only a copy is sent to the monitoring device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** port-mirroring, span, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a network tap?
**A:** A physical device connected between two devices to capture traffic
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** network-tap, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a network tap and port mirroring?
**A:** A tap is a physical device placed inline between two devices; port mirroring uses a switch to copy traffic to a monitoring port — both gather raw packets
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** network-tap, port-mirroring, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between NetFlow data and protocol analyzer data?
**A:** NetFlow provides a summary of statistics based on packets; a protocol analyzer shows the actual raw packets (exact bits and bytes)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** netflow, protocol-analyzer, monitoring, domain-3, prof-messer
