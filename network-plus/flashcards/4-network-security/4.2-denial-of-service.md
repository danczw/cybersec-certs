# Denial of Service Flashcards

**Q:** What is a denial of service (DoS)?
**A:** An action or series of actions that causes a service to fail, often by overloading a system so no resources are available to legitimate users
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name two methods an attacker can use to cause a DoS.
**A:** Overwhelm server capabilities with traffic, or exploit a vulnerability in an OS/application to cause failure
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why might an attacker use a DoS as a distraction?
**A:** To keep troubleshooting resources busy on one server while attacking another part of the network
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is keeping systems up to date with security patches important for preventing DoS?
**A:** Attackers can pinpoint vulnerabilities in unpatched systems and cause them to fail relatively easily
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give an example of a physical denial of service.
**A:** Pulling the main power for an entire facility
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can connecting switches without spanning tree protocol cause a DoS?
**A:** It creates a network loop — traffic multiplies and overwhelms switch capacity
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** dos, accidental, stp, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name three ways a DoS can occur accidentally.
**A:** Network loop from switches without STP, downloading a large file on limited bandwidth, physical environment issue (water/roof leak in data center)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** dos, accidental, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does DDoS stand for?
**A:** Distributed Denial of Service
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ddos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What makes a DDoS different from a regular DoS?
**A:** Multiple devices act in unison from different locations to cause the denial of service, rather than a single source
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ddos, dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a botnet's role in a DDoS attack?
**A:** It takes over millions of personal computers and directs all their traffic at one target server
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ddos, botnet, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an asymmetric threat?
**A:** When an attacker uses very few resources to bring down systems with many more resources
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ddos, asymmetric, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is a DDoS considered an asymmetric threat?
**A:** Devices with very limited internet access can combine to overwhelm systems with much greater resources
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ddos, asymmetric, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is DDoS reflection and amplification?
**A:** Attacker sends a small amount of information; internet protocols multiply it into much larger traffic directed at the victim
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ddos, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is DDoS amplification popular with attackers?
**A:** It doesn't require many resources from the attacker to overwhelm a remote host
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ddos, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name three protocols that can be used for DDoS amplification.
**A:** NTP (Network Time Protocol), DNS (Domain Name System), ICMP (Internet Control Message Protocol)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In DNS amplification, what makes the response much larger than the query?
**A:** Using the ANY parameter returns extensive data (e.g., DNSSEC key information) rather than just an IP address
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, dns, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In DNS amplification, what is the ratio of query size to response size?
**A:** A 28-byte query can produce a 1,300-byte response
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, dns, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the five steps in a DNS amplification DDoS attack?
**A:** 1) Command and control sends instructions to botnet, 2) Botnet sends small DNS queries to open resolvers, 3) Botnet spoofs the source address to the victim's IP, 4) Resolvers send amplified responses to victim, 5) Amplified traffic overwhelms victim's resources
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, dns, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do botnet devices spoof the source address during DNS amplification?
**A:** So the amplified DNS responses are sent to the victim's IP address instead of back to the botnet
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, dns, amplification, spoofing, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the role of open DNS resolvers in a DDoS amplification attack?
**A:** They receive small queries from the botnet and return much larger responses, which are directed at the victim due to spoofed source addresses
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ddos, dns, amplification, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Who has been identified as the source of some DoS attacks against businesses?
**A:** Competitors — seeking to make rival services unavailable
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** dos, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer
