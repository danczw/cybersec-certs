# MAC Flooding Flashcards

**Q:** What does MAC stand for?
**A:** Media Access Control
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a MAC address?
**A:** The hardware address of an ethernet adapter — unique per device, allowing traffic to be sent specifically to one device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the format of a MAC address?
**A:** 48 bits (6 bytes), displayed as hexadecimal with colon or period delimiters
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the first 3 bytes of a MAC address called, and what do they identify?
**A:** OUI (Organizationally Unique Identifier) — identifies the manufacturer of the network adapter
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, oui, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What do the last 3 bytes of a MAC address represent?
**A:** The NIC-specific value — effectively the serial number of that particular adapter
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where is a MAC address stored on a network adapter?
**A:** In ROM (Read-Only Memory) — referred to as the burned-in address
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, fundamentals, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a MAC address table on a switch?
**A:** A table mapping MAC addresses to switch interfaces, so the switch knows which port to use to reach each device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac, switch, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does a switch build its MAC address table?
**A:** By examining the source MAC address of inbound frames and recording which interface they arrived on
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, switch, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How long do entries remain in a switch's MAC address table?
**A:** Approximately 5 minutes — entries are temporary and must be relearned after expiring
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, switch, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when a switch receives a frame with a destination MAC that IS in its table?
**A:** The switch forwards the frame only out the mapped interface — other devices do not receive it
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, switch, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What happens when a switch receives a frame with a destination MAC that is NOT in its table?
**A:** The switch floods the frame to every interface (except the one it arrived on)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, switch, flooding, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why does a switch flood frames with unknown destination MACs?
**A:** To guarantee traffic always reaches its destination, even if the switch hasn't yet learned where that MAC is located
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac, switch, flooding, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a MAC flooding attack?
**A:** Attacker sends many frames with different random source MAC addresses to fill the switch's MAC address table, causing all traffic to be flooded to every port
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac-flooding, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the effect of a successful MAC flooding attack?
**A:** The switch effectively becomes a hub — all inbound frames are forwarded to every interface, allowing the attacker to capture all traffic
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac-flooding, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why does filling the MAC address table cause the switch to flood all traffic?
**A:** With the table full of garbage entries, legitimate destination MACs can't be found, triggering the normal behavior of flooding to all interfaces
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** mac-flooding, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What normal switch behavior does MAC flooding exploit?
**A:** The default behavior of forwarding frames to all interfaces when the destination MAC is not found in the table
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac-flooding, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the mitigation for MAC flooding?
**A:** Port security — limits how many MAC addresses can be learned on a single interface, making it much harder to fill the table
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** mac-flooding, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What protocol uses MAC addresses to maintain network uptime and avoid loops?
**A:** STP (Spanning Tree Protocol)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac, stp, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a switch and a hub in terms of traffic forwarding?
**A:** A switch forwards frames only to the specific interface mapped to the destination MAC; a hub forwards all frames to every interface
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** mac-flooding, switch, domain-4, prof-messer
