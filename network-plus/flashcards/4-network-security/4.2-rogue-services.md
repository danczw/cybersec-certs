# Rogue Services Flashcards

**Q:** Why is DHCP vulnerable to rogue servers?
**A:** No security is built into the DHCP protocol — any device on the network can respond to DHCP requests
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rogue-dhcp, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What damage can a rogue DHCP server cause?
**A:** Duplicate IP addresses, invalid IPs, loss of internet connectivity, and effective network shutdown
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-dhcp, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is DHCP snooping?
**A:** An enterprise switch feature that examines all DHCP requests and only allows responses from legitimate DHCP servers
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-dhcp, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does Microsoft Active Directory help prevent rogue DHCP servers?
**A:** AD determines which DHCP servers are authorized to hand out IP addresses and only allows those servers to respond
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-dhcp, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What three steps should you take after finding a rogue DHCP server?
**A:** Remove it from the network, eliminate it from responding to DHCP requests, then renew all IP addresses to ensure everyone has a legitimate IP
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-dhcp, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a rogue access point?
**A:** An unauthorized access point plugged into any ethernet connection on the network — may not be malicious (employee expanding coverage) but still creates security issues
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** rogue-ap, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Besides a physical AP, how else can a rogue access point be created?
**A:** Using an OS wireless sharing feature that turns an entire computer into an access point
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-ap, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name two methods for detecting rogue access points.
**A:** Periodic network scans, and physically walking the facility with a wireless analyzer
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-ap, detection, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How does 802.1X (Network Access Control) mitigate rogue access points?
**A:** It requires authentication before granting network access — even if a rogue AP is plugged in, users can't access the network without proper credentials
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-ap, 802.1x, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a wireless evil twin?
**A:** A malicious rogue access point designed to look exactly like legitimate APs at a location, combining phishing techniques with wireless networking
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** evil-twin, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four characteristics of a wireless evil twin.
**A:** Same or similar SSID, similar security settings, may duplicate captive portal configuration, increased radio output power to overpower legitimate APs
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** evil-twin, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why do evil twins increase their radio output power?
**A:** To overpower legitimate access points in the area and become the primary AP that devices connect to
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** evil-twin, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the mitigation for a wireless evil twin even if you connect to one?
**A:** Always use encrypted traffic (VPN or HTTPS) — encrypted data can't be read even if the attacker intercepts it
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** evil-twin, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an on-path attack?
**A:** An attacker sits in the middle of a conversation between two devices, receives and examines information, possibly changes it, then forwards it — also called man in the middle
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** on-path, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** In an on-path attack, do the source and destination know the attacker is there?
**A:** No — neither side has any idea the attacker is in the middle or that data was changed
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** on-path, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name five types of on-path attacks.
**A:** Wireless evil twin, ARP poisoning, session hijacking, HTTPS spoofing, Wi-Fi eavesdropping
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** on-path, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the general mitigation for on-path attacks?
**A:** Encrypt all data — even if the attacker is in the middle, they cannot read the information being transferred
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** on-path, mitigation, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a rogue access point and a wireless evil twin?
**A:** A rogue AP is any unauthorized access point (may not be malicious); a wireless evil twin is specifically malicious and designed to impersonate legitimate APs
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rogue-ap, evil-twin, attacks, domain-4, prof-messer
