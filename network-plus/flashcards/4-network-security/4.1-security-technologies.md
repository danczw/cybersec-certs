# Security Technologies Flashcards

**Q:** What is a honeypot?
**A:** A virtual network with virtual servers/components designed to attract attackers so their behavior and techniques can be observed
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** honeypot, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Are attacks on honeypots typically performed by humans interactively?
**A:** No — attacks are usually automated scripts/programs targeting many systems simultaneously
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** honeypot, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the constant challenge with honeypots?
**A:** Attackers are good at detecting what's real vs. fake, so honeypots must be continually improved to remain convincing
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** honeypot, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a honeynet?
**A:** A larger deception framework combining multiple honeypot components (servers, workstations, routers, firewalls, switches, proxy servers, NAS) to simulate a real network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** honeynet, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a honeypot and a honeynet?
**A:** A honeypot is a single virtual device/server; a honeynet combines many components (servers, workstations, routers, firewalls, switches, NAS) into a larger deception framework
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** honeypot, honeynet, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why does a honeynet include multiple types of devices?
**A:** To make the environment seem real enough for attackers to move laterally, increasing the chance they engage with the deception
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** honeynet, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Are honeypots/honeynets typically physical or virtual devices?
**A:** Commonly virtual devices
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** honeypot, honeynet, deception, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is risk in IT security?
**A:** Exposure to something harmful or dangerous; describes how possible it is for something bad to happen
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** risk, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Does risk increase or decrease as an organization grows?
**A:** Risk tends to increase as the organization grows larger
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** risk, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four situations where risk should be considered.
**A:** Expanding the organization, adding new applications, making configuration changes, or anything that might open the organization to attack
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** risk, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the two business decisions that can be made after identifying risk?
**A:** Continue with the task as-is, or add additional security controls to help protect it
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** risk, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a vulnerability?
**A:** A weakness in a system (operating system, application) that could allow unauthorized access to systems or data if exploited
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** vulnerability, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can vulnerabilities exist without being discovered?
**A:** Yes — undiscovered vulnerabilities may have existed for months or years before being found
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vulnerability, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name four types of vulnerabilities.
**A:** Data injection, authentication flaws, exposed data, security misconfiguration
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vulnerability, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an exploit?
**A:** When someone takes advantage of a vulnerability to gain access to a system or data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** exploit, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a vulnerability and an exploit?
**A:** A vulnerability is a weakness; an exploit is the act of using that weakness to gain unauthorized access
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** vulnerability, exploit, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can exploits vary in complexity?
**A:** Yes — can range from straightforward (e.g., embedded username in code) to complex multi-step attacks
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** exploit, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a threat?
**A:** What is used by an attacker to exploit a vulnerability; can be intentional (attacker) or accidental (fire, flood)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** threat, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Can threats be accidental?
**A:** Yes — fire, flood, and other natural disasters are examples of accidental threats
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** threat, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Where do most internet-based threats originate?
**A:** Outside the organization
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** threat, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Describe the four-step threat process from vulnerability to impact.
**A:** 1) OS has a vulnerability → 2) Threat agent creates a threat action → 3) Threat action exploits the vulnerability → 4) Result: system unavailability, data breach, or data exposure
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** threat, vulnerability, exploit, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the three possible results of a successful exploit?
**A:** System unavailability, data breach, or data exposure
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** exploit, threat, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does the CIA triad stand for?
**A:** Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cia-triad, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is the CIA triad sometimes called the AIC triad?
**A:** To differentiate it from the CIA (Central Intelligence Agency) of the US federal government
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cia-triad, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does Confidentiality mean in the CIA triad?
**A:** Protecting data from unauthorized access — achieved by restricting access or encrypting data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cia-triad, confidentiality, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Name two methods for achieving confidentiality.
**A:** Restricting access to data and encrypting data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cia-triad, confidentiality, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does Integrity mean in the CIA triad?
**A:** Data has not been modified by unauthorized third parties; messages across the network can't be modified without detection
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cia-triad, integrity, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What security mechanism is commonly associated with integrity?
**A:** Digital signatures
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cia-triad, integrity, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does Availability mean in the CIA triad?
**A:** Systems and data remain accessible to authorized users; security measures must not impair overall system access
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cia-triad, availability, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which leg of the CIA triad is violated if an attacker encrypts your files with ransomware?
**A:** Availability — authorized users can no longer access their data
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** cia-triad, availability, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which leg of the CIA triad is violated if an attacker intercepts and alters a message in transit?
**A:** Integrity — the data was modified by an unauthorized third party
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** cia-triad, integrity, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Which leg of the CIA triad is violated if sensitive data is leaked to the public?
**A:** Confidentiality — unauthorized parties gained access to protected data
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** cia-triad, confidentiality, security-concepts, domain-4, prof-messer
