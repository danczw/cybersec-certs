# Security Concepts Flashcards

**Q:** What is data in transit (data in-motion)?
**A:** Information transferred over a wired or wireless network
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** data-states, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the primary function of network infrastructure devices (switches, routers) regarding data?
**A:** Forward traffic to the next location — they are not designed to protect data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** data-states, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What devices provide security for data in transit?
**A:** Firewalls and intrusion prevention systems — they monitor traffic, decide to forward or block, and inspect for malicious content
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** data-states, security, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are two common encryption types for data in transit?
**A:** TLS (Transport Layer Security) and IPsec (Internet Protocol Security)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** data-states, encryption, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is data at rest?
**A:** Information saved to a hard drive, SSD, or any other storage device
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** data-states, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are three types of data at rest encryption?
**A:** Full disk encryption (entire disk), database encryption (single part or entire database), and file/folder encryption (individual files or folders)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** data-states, encryption, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are ACLs in the context of data at rest security?
**A:** Access Control Lists — determine which users have access or no access to data; controlled by system administrator or data owner
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** acl, data-states, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is PKI (Public Key Infrastructure)?
**A:** The policies and control of all encryption keys and certificates for an organization
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** pki, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When do organizations typically begin building out their PKI?
**A:** When they start adding encryption keys to web servers or signing certificates to users or devices
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** pki, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What does a digital certificate do?
**A:** Assigns a level of trust to a user or device by binding a public key with a digital signature and details about the key holder (name, organization, etc.)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** certificates, pki, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What information does a digital certificate bind together?
**A:** A public key, a digital signature, and details about the key holder (name, organization, etc.)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** certificates, pki, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Who digitally signs certificates to add trust?
**A:** A central Certificate Authority (CA)
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** ca, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a web of trust?
**A:** A distributed form of trust (alternative to a centralized CA) — if A trusts B and B trusts C, then A can also trust C
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** web-of-trust, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is a Certificate Authority (CA)?
**A:** A centralized authority that all certificates start from — anything signed by the CA can be trusted anywhere in the organization
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ca, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the two options for deploying a Certificate Authority?
**A:** Internal CA (organization builds and self-signs its own certificates) or third-party CA (independent authority, useful for internet-facing trust)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ca, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How can anyone verify a digital certificate is trustworthy?
**A:** Examine the certificate, see who signed it (the CA), and determine if that CA's signature is trusted
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** ca, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** When would you use a third-party CA vs. an internal CA?
**A:** Third-party CA for internet-facing trust (anyone in the world can reference it). Internal CA when all devices and users are within your own organization.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** ca, certificates, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is IAM (Identity and Access Management)?
**A:** The process of managing permissions and access to data
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** iam, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are the four components of IAM?
**A:** Access control (users access only what they need), authentication (verify identity), authorization (grant permissions once authenticated), auditing (track who accessed data and when)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** iam, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is the principle of least privilege?
**A:** Users only have the rights and permissions necessary for their job function — no additional access is granted
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** least-privilege, iam, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Why is administrator access not assigned to every system?
**A:** Least privilege — everyone having admin access would allow anyone to access any data at any time, creating a significant security concern
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** least-privilege, iam, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)?
**A:** Separating individuals into roles (e.g., staff, manager, VP), with the administrator defining permissions per role and assigning users to appropriate roles
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rbac, iam, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How is RBAC implemented in Windows?
**A:** Through groups — one group per role, users assigned to those groups
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** rbac, iam, windows, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What three methods can determine a user's geographic location?
**A:** IP address (not always accurate), GPS (Global Positioning System), and wireless network name / access point location
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** geofencing, geographic, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is geofencing?
**A:** Allowing or disallowing access to information based on a user's physical location
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** geofencing, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Give an example of geofencing in practice.
**A:** Sensitive data only viewable from within the corporate headquarters building — if outside the building, access is denied
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** geofencing, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** How might VPN users experience geographic restrictions?
**A:** They may get different permissions based on their connection location (e.g., different state or country)
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** geofencing, vpn, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is CCTV?
**A:** Closed Circuit Television — cameras deployed around buildings/campus, networked to central storage for long-term retention
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Tags:** cctv, physical-security, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What modern features do CCTV cameras offer beyond basic video?
**A:** Motion detection, license plate reading, and facial recognition
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** cctv, physical-security, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What are five types of door lock access methods?
**A:** Physical key (conventional lock/deadbolt), PIN code (electronic reader), RFID badge (token-based), biometric (handprint, fingerprint, retina scan), and multi-factor (combining methods)
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** door-locks, physical-security, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** What is an example of multi-factor authentication for physical access?
**A:** Using a badge to badge in, plus entering a PIN — prevents unauthorized access if someone finds a lost badge
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Tags:** door-locks, mfa, physical-security, domain-4, prof-messer

---

**Q:** Compare data in transit vs. data at rest.
**A:** In transit: data moving over network, secured by firewalls/IPS and TLS/IPsec. At rest: data on storage, secured by disk/database/file encryption and ACLs.
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Tags:** data-states, comparison, domain-4, prof-messer
