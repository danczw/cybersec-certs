Lesson 0002 · Network+ N10-009

Reading an IPv4 Address on Sight

One table, twelve reps. Name any address in under two seconds.

~8 minutes Objective 1.7 Pays into domains 2 and 5

You scored 33% on Networking Concepts in the diagnostic — your weakest domain, and all three questions were IPv4 addressing or ports. You also expanded only one acronym before answering, which rules out vocabulary as the cause. So the gap is conceptual, and this is where it starts.

This is the highest-leverage thing you can fix right now, for a reason worth knowing: address recognition is not really a domain 1 skill. It is the first move in nearly every troubleshooting question on the exam — and troubleshooting is 24% of it, your other weak area. A symptom like "the host has 169.254.12.9" has already told you the answer, if you can read it.

The whole skill

Read the first octet. It settles almost every address by itself. Only 172 and 192 need a second glance — and that is exactly where the exam sets its traps.

First octetThen checkVerdict
10—Private
127—Loopback
169is second octet 254?Yes → link-local · No → public
172is second octet 16–31?Yes → private · No → public
192is second octet 168?Yes → private · No → public
224–239—Multicast
240–255—Reserved
anything else—Public

Why 16–31 and not 16–32. 172.16.0.0/12 leaves 20 host bits, which is sixteen consecutive /16 networks: 172.16 through 172.31. The mask does not land on an octet boundary, which is precisely why this one is worth memorising as a flat fact instead of re-deriving it under time pressure.

The two traps

172.32.0.1 is public — the private block stops at 172.31. 192.167.1.1 is public — only 192.168 is private. Both read as private at a glance, and that glance is the thing being tested. You missed the /12 boundary question in the diagnostic, so expect both of these below.

Twelve reps

Same four labels on most questions, deliberately. You are not learning new facts here — you are making one lookup automatic, and that takes repetition against varied input. Say your answer out loud before you click; a silent guess does not build anything.
