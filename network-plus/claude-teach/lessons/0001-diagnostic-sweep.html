Diagnostic Sweep

Fifteen questions to find out where you actually stand.

~10 minutes All five domains Retrieval practice

You have 86 note files and 82 flashcard decks in this repo. Coverage is not your problem. The open question is retention — and you cannot answer that by re-reading notes, because re-reading produces fluency: the warm feeling of recognising material you could not actually reproduce under exam conditions.

This is the fluency/storage distinction. Fluency is in-the-moment recognition; storage strength is durable retrieval. Re-reading inflates the first and barely touches the second. Testing yourself builds the second directly — the testing effect, one of the most replicated findings in learning science. See Agarwal & Roediger, How to Use Retrieval Practice to Improve Learning.

So: no studying first. Answer cold. A wrong answer here is worth more than a right one — it marks exactly where your study time should go, and the act of guessing wrong then seeing the correction encodes the fix better than reading it would have.

How this works

One shot per question — once you click, the answer locks. Feedback appears immediately, with the repo file the question came from so you can go read the source. Your progress saves, so you can stop and come back.

Acronyms

You mentioned you often have to look the acronyms up first. Every acronym in this lesson is clickable — give yourself one beat to recall it, then click. All 234 expansions come from abbreviations.md, and each one links back to the note that introduced it.

Clicks are counted, and that count is the point. A decent score with many lookups means the vocabulary is your bottleneck, which is a far cheaper problem to fix than shaky concepts — so the result panel reports both numbers separately. Don't ration the clicks to look good; an unlooked-up guess tells us nothing.

The sweep

Questions are weighted roughly to the real exam: Networking Concepts 23%, Implementation 20%, Operations 19%, Security 14%, Troubleshooting 24%. Domains are interleaved rather than blocked, so you have to work out which body of knowledge applies before you can answer — closer to the real exam, and harder in the way that helps.
